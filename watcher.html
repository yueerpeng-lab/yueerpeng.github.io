Watcher — Consumer Subtitle Review

The consumer-facing companion to HAMM. Watcher lets reviewers select a title, upload a video, and produce AI-generated subtitles ready for download — a streamlined flow built for non-technical users contributing to Disney's localisation pipeline.

Role

Product Intern · UI Design + QA

Type

Consumer SaaS · Material-style web app

Period

Feb – Jul 2025

Tools

Figma · Jira · Annotation tools

01 / Overview

Localisation, but for everyone.

Disney's subtitle pipeline isn't only for production professionals. Reviewers, consultants, and external partners also need to validate AI-generated subtitles — ideally without learning a complex enterprise platform.

Watcher is the entry point for that audience. A familiar Material Design language, single-purpose forms, and a clear primary action keep the interaction approachable. Power-user controls (regional defaults, auto-generated metadata, batch export) sit one click away, surfaced only when relevant.

Design Direction

Familiar Material patterns + Disney's signature blue. New users should recognise the platform within seconds.

02 / Approach

One screen, one decision.

Each step of the workflow is a single screen with one primary action. The "Select Title" page is empty until the user types — there's no decoy content, no over-promising. The Floating Action Button (the round blue plus) is the only affordance pulling the eye forward.

Why Material?

Disney's reviewers come from diverse backgrounds — translators, consultants, part-time contractors. Many are already fluent in Google Workspace, YouTube Studio, Android. Material's predictable behaviours (sticky FABs, snackbars, dropdowns) reduced the onboarding burden compared to a custom design language.

What I owned

  • UI design from blank page through interactive Figma prototype.
  • Component spec and hand-off to engineering.
  • Functional testing of AI subtitle output, including edge cases (multi-speaker, music intervals, on-screen text).
  • Annotation work — labelling subtitle errors to feed back into the model.
  • Coordination with Singapore and Japan QA teams via daily Teams sync.

03 / Reflection

Designing for second-time users.

Most enterprise tools optimise for the power user. Watcher had to optimise for someone who'd opened it twice in their life and might not return for weeks. That meant fighting against complexity at every step — defaulting where possible, surfacing helpful context only when needed, and trusting the conventions of Material Design rather than reinventing them.

Working alongside HAMM also gave me a comparative perspective on dark vs. light tools, enterprise vs. consumer flows, and the small but consequential differences in language, density, and rhythm between the two.
