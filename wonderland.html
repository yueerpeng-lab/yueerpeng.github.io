Wonderland — Educational Concept Design

A year-long creative-design engagement with Wonderland (未来学习社), a Shanghai-based company designing innovative educational concepts and curricula. From brochures and exhibition key visuals to spatial installations and device interfaces — every artefact centred on making creativity tangible for young learners.

Role

Creative Designer

Type

Brand, print, spatial, and exhibition design

Period

Nov 2023 – Oct 2024

Tools

Illustrator · InDesign · After Effects · Figma

01 / Context

Designing the experience of creative education.

Wonderland builds educational curricula and experiences for middle-school students, partnering with schools across Shanghai. Their programmes aren't traditional classrooms — they're immersive, hands-on creative sessions designed to teach through making.

My role was to make these programmes visible: to create the visual artefacts — brochures, manuals, exhibition graphics, spatial installations — that communicate the energy and intent of the learning experience before a student ever walks through the door.

02 / Scope of Work

From page to space.

Project brochures & decks

Programme overviews for partner schools and parents. Each brochure needed to balance institutional trust with the playful, creative tone of the educational content itself.

Creative-education manuals

Curriculum-adjacent manuals for middle-school students — part activity guide, part design workbook. Layout, illustration, and print production from concept through final handoff.

Exhibition key visuals

Main visual identities for student exhibition events. These set the emotional tone for the space and were reproduced across banners, wayfinding, social media, and invitations.

Spatial installations & wall design

Physical design within exhibition and learning spaces — wall graphics, modular installations, and spatial arrangements that turn a bare venue into an environment that feels like the programme.

Range

Over 12 months, deliverables spanned print, digital, spatial, and identity work — each piece connected by a visual language rooted in geometric playfulness, bold colour, and generous whitespace.

03 / Approach

A visual language built from shapes.

Wonderland's brand is built on four primary shapes — circle, rotated square, triangle, and smaller circle — each in a distinct colour. These primitives are simple enough for children to reproduce, distinctive enough to be recognised across formats, and flexible enough to anchor a brochure cover or a five-metre wall graphic.

I extended these shapes into every deliverable: as section markers in manuals, wayfinding elements in spatial design, and compositional anchors in exhibition key visuals. The constraint turned out to be generative — a limited vocabulary forced more creative combinations.

04 / Reflection

What a year of production teaches.

A twelve-month engagement is different from a semester project. Wonderland gave me sustained production experience: real deadlines, real print runs, real installations that had to survive a hundred students walking through them.

It also taught me something about visual systems: the best ones aren't clever, they're durable. The four-shape vocabulary worked because it could absorb new contexts (a new exhibition theme, a different school's brand guidelines) without breaking.

That principle — design for durability over novelty — has shaped my thinking about component systems and design tokens in every product context since.
